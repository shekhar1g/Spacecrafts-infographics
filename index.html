Interstellar travelers

Five spacecraft leaving the solar system

Only five human-made objects have ever reached the speed to escape the Sun's gravity for good. Here is when each one launched, how far it has gone, where it is headed, and why it was sent.

How far they are

Distance from the Sun in astronomical units (AU). One AU is the distance from Earth to the Sun, about 93 million miles. Distances are approximate and grow every day.

Where they are headed

The five paths point in very different directions, so none of these spacecraft will ever meet. This side view shows the flat plane the planets orbit in, with the three craft whose tilt angles are known.

    Schematic, not to scale in angle or distance detail. Tilt angles for the Pioneers are not drawn here. Directions describe where each path points today. The spacecraft are not steering toward these stars, and getting near them would take tens of thousands to millions of years.

    How fast they travel

    Cruising speed relative to the Sun, in miles per hour. The gold line marks New Horizons' launch speed.

    New Horizons left Earth faster than any of the five, at about 36,400 mph, but it got only one planetary gravity assist, from Jupiter. The Voyagers got several slingshots, which is why they cruise faster today, and New Horizons will never catch up.

    Why they were sent

    Each spacecraft had its own job, and most outlived their plans by decades.

    A few more things worth knowing

    48 hrs

    The round trip for a radio message to Voyager 1 once it passes one light-day, 24 hours out and 24 hours back.

    1 million mi

    About how far Voyager 1 travels every single day.

    4 watts

    Roughly how much electrical power each Voyager loses every year as its plutonium power source fades.

    300 years

    NASA's estimate for Voyager 1 to reach the inner edge of the Oort Cloud. Crossing it takes about 30,000 years.

    40,000 years

    When Voyager 1 passes about 1.7 light-years from the star Gliese 445.

    2 plaques

    The Pioneers each carry a gold-anodized plaque showing human figures and a star map of Earth's location.

    Distances, speeds and statuses are approximate as of November 2026. Figures for Voyager 1 and 2 come from NASA and JPL. Pioneer 10, Pioneer 11 and New Horizons distances and the speeds are rounded values from research notes. Pioneer 10 is described as likely in interstellar space because it can no longer report measurements.
